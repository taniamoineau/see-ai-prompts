Prompts to try

Copy a prompt, paste it into your AI assistant and replace everything in [brackets] with your own details.

Before you start

  1. The prompts work in Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini. Some steps need features your tool or plan may not have, such as scheduled tasks or access to your email.
  2. Check your company’s AI policy before connecting a work mailbox or uploading work files. When in doubt, test with dummy data.
  3. Read what comes back before you use or send it.
1. Global instructions 2. Email triage 3. Selling story slides 4. Weekly news digest

1.Global instructions for accurate answers

Set this up once. Your assistant then follows these rules in every chat.

Where to paste it: the custom or personal instructions in your assistant’s settings.

Prompt
You are committed to truth and accuracy above everything else, including being helpful. A wrong answer delivered confidently is worse than no answer. Follow these 7 rules in every response:

1. UNCERTAINTY: If you are not fully certain about something, say so clearly. Use phrases like "I am not certain, but..." or "You may want to verify this...". Never state guesses as facts.

2. SOURCES: Do not invent paper titles, author names, URLs, or book references. If you cannot name a real, verifiable source, say "I do not have a verified source for this."

3. STATISTICS: Flag any number you are not 100 percent confident in. Say "approximately" and recommend I verify it from a primary source.

4. RECENT EVENTS: Remind me when a topic may have changed since your knowledge cutoff. Do not present outdated info as current.

5. PEOPLE and QUOTES: Never attribute a quote to a real person unless you are certain they said it. If unsure, say "I cannot confirm this quote is accurate."

6. CODE and TECHNICAL: Never invent function names, library methods, or API syntax. If unsure a function exists, tell me to verify it in the current docs.

7. LOGIC GAPS: Do not fill missing context with assumptions. If something is unclear, ask a clarifying question before answering.

If a response would require breaking any of these rules, choose honesty over helpfulness every time.

2.Email triage

Sort your inbox by urgency and get draft replies to the urgent emails.

You need: your email connected to the assistant, or paste the emails into the chat.

Prompt
Review my unread emails and triage them by urgency into these four categories:

1. URGENT: Action needed today. Time-sensitive items, hard deadlines, supply chain risks (stockouts, shipment delays), or operational blocks where someone is waiting on me.
2. Action needed: I need to reply, approve, review or do something, but not today.
3. Need to be informed: Updates I should know about but don't need to act on.
4. Newsletters and other info: Newsletters, notifications, promotions and automated alerts.

For each email, give the sender, the subject, a one-line summary, and the deadline or required action if there is one. List the most critical items first within each category. If a deadline may already have passed, say so.

Then draft a reply to each URGENT email and show the drafts for my review. Do not send anything. Use [brackets] for anything I need to confirm, such as times or quantities.

If an email's urgency is unclear, put it in the higher category and tell me why.

3.Selling story slides from your reports

Turn market and shopper reports into a 2-slide selling story for a retailer, with every number traced to its source.

You need: the reports attached to the chat, or a connected folder.

Download sample reports (zip, about 70 MB)

Prompt
Goal: Build a 2-slide selling story to convince [retailer] to list [your product] in [market].
Sources: Use ONLY the reports in [the attached files / the connected folder]. Do not use outside knowledge, the web, or assumptions. Do not invent, round up, or extrapolate any number, trend, or claim.
Process:

1. Read every report fully. Make a short list of the files you reviewed.
2. Extract the most relevant evidence for a retailer: demand and growth in [your category], shopper and consumer needs, category size and share, margin or basket value, trends, competitor gaps, and anything on the target channel or market.
3. Build an evidence log (table) where each row has: ID (E1, E2...), the fact or figure as stated in the report, the report name, and the page or section. Quote figures exactly.
4. Propose a storyline in 3-5 lines: the problem or opportunity for the retailer, the evidence, and the ask. Show it to me before building the slides.
5. Turn the storyline into 2 slides:
   * Slide 1, “The opportunity”: a headline that states the insight, 3-4 supporting data points, and one simple chart or visual if the data supports it.
   * Slide 2, “Why this launch and what we propose”: a headline, the logic for why this product fits the retailer’s shoppers, and a clear next step or ask.
6. Put an evidence reference on every claim, using the evidence log IDs in a small footnote (for example, [E3]). Put the full source list (report, page) in the speaker notes or a short appendix.

Rules:

* If the reports don’t contain evidence for a point I’d normally make, say so in a “Gaps” list. Do not fill the gap.
* If two reports disagree, show both and flag the conflict.
* Keep slide text concise: no paragraphs, one message per slide.
* Tone: confident, fact-based, retailer-focused.

Final check: Before delivering, re-verify every number on the slides against the evidence log and the original report. List any item you couldn’t verify.

4.Weekly MENA FMCG and retail news digest

A short news digest every Monday, delivered to your inbox.

You need: a tool that can search the web, run scheduled tasks and send email. Without those, run it by hand and copy the result.

Prompt
Every Monday morning, research the last 7 days of news in FMCG and retail across the MENA region (GCC, Egypt, Levant, North Africa). Cover: brand and retailer announcements, earnings and M&A, pricing and commodity moves, supply chain and logistics, consumer trends, e-commerce and quick commerce, retail media, and regulatory or food-safety changes.

Sources. Use any credible source, including news sites, trade press, company announcements, blogs, social media and aggregators. Prefer primary or original reporting, and cross-check claims that rely on social media, blogs or aggregators before including them. If a claim can’t be confirmed, leave it out.

Date check. Include only items published within the last 7 days. Open each article to confirm its date. If fewer than 6 items qualify, include only those and say so in one line at the top.

Format. English only. Title it “MENA FMCG & Retail Weekly” with a 3-line “Top story,” then up to 8 items grouped by theme (Brands, Retail & E-commerce, Markets & Costs, Regulation). Each item gets a 2-sentence summary, why it matters, the publication date and a source link. End with a one-line “Watch next week.” Keep it under 500 words, scannable, no hype. Skip empty sections.

Delivery. Email it to [email] with the subject “MENA FMCG & Retail Weekly: [date]”.

Change the region, topics or format to fit your category. The date check and the instruction to leave out unconfirmed claims are what keep the digest reliable.
